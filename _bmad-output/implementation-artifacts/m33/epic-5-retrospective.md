# M33 Epic 5 Retrospective

## Outcome

The customer Workbench now exposes one exact product surface, `Cabinet`, while Documentation,
Schematic, and Wiring remain hidden compatibility projections. Create Device stays only because the
real Electron path proves governed preview, acceptance, projection, source persistence, nested ports,
and reopen. Internal proof controls no longer consume normal toolbar space.

## Evidence

- Frontend regression: `214/214`.
- Theia product build: browser, node, and Electron builds completed with zero errors.
- Electron: one `Cabinet` surface; Cabinet survives refresh; toolbar has four normal commands, one
  inspection affordance, and zero proof/debug controls.
- Create Device: accepted backend-authority mutation remains visible in Cabinet and survives reopen.

## Lessons

1. Product-surface naming cannot be inferred from whichever backend projection is active.
2. Compatibility views must not become peer customer UX merely because transport support remains.
3. Evidence belongs in typed payloads and inspection, not permanent toolbar controls.
4. A control is allowed in the demo only when the complete transaction is proven; unavailable
   capability must fail closed rather than expose placeholder UI.
5. Workbench density and drawing quality are separate acceptance boundaries. Epic 5 fixed the first;
   Epic 6 must still replace legacy box rendering and sparse composition.

## Carry Forward

- Wire M33 Graphic Primitive IR and composition facts into the live Cabinet projection.
- Reject generic boxes, center-anchor fallback, duplicate/off-screen content, and fixed oversized
  viewBoxes in the M33 product smoke.
- Do not claim customer-demo readiness until desktop and narrow Electron screenshots are inspected.
